A place as a person decides on it: its photo and kind, its rating and price level, how far it is,
and whether it is open — worked out from its hours at the moment the card is drawn, across
midnight, on the place's own clock when the data gives its offset. Open the frame at another time
of day and the line changes.

## Usage

```bash
npm install @aparte/plugin-cards @aparte/core
```

```ts
import { setupPlaceCard } from '@aparte/plugin-cards/place';

setupPlaceCard({ tool: 'find_places', currencySymbol: '€' });
```

A price level is a tier, not an amount: `currencySymbol` is the sign it is drawn with (`$` by
default). The reader takes both generations of Google Places and Yelp; the fields and the hours
helpers are in [the guide](/guides/cards/#places--place).

## A real map

Without a photo, the card draws a map of its own and fetches nothing. Give it your map tiles and
a place with coordinates sits on a real one, under its marker:

```ts
setupPlaceCard({
  tool: 'find_places',
  mapTiles: 'https://tile.openstreetmap.org/{z}/{x}/{y}.png',
  mapAttribution: '© OpenStreetMap contributors',
});
```

- **Your provider, your choice.** Any server that cuts tiles the usual way: OpenStreetMap's, with
  no key but [a usage policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles/) that rules out
  heavy traffic; MapTiler's or Stadia's, with your key in the address; your own. Each tile is a
  request from your visitor's browser to that server, which sees the visitor and the place —
  which is why the card fetches none unless you ask.
- **The credit.** `mapAttribution` is shown in a corner of the map; most providers require one.
- **How close.** `mapZoom` (16 by default) is the tile server's zoom: 16 is a street and its
  neighbours, 13 a district.
- **What stays.** A photo still wins over the map. A place without coordinates keeps the drawn
  map, and so does a card whose tile fails to load. The dark scheme keeps the tiles as they are;
  `--aparte-place-map-filter` takes a CSS filter to dim them (`brightness(0.85)`).
