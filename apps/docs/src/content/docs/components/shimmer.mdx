`.aparte-shimmer` goes on the span that holds the words of a step in progress: "Searching the
web…", "Thinking". A light sweeps across the letters, which is what conversational chats show
where an agent's row shows a spinner and a word. Take the class off when the step ends; the text
stays the same element.

The chat uses it in three places: the [reasoning](/components/reasoning/) line while it arrives,
a [quiet tool row](/components/tool/#a-line-for-a-person) while its call runs, and a
conversation's title in the [conversation list](/components/conversation-list/) while it answers.

## Customize

<Vars />

The sweep runs from the muted ink to the full ink, two text colours whose order holds in both
themes. `--aparte-duration-shimmer` is the length of one sweep.

## Accessibility

- The words are real text: a screen reader reads them, and the element keeps its colour for
  everything that reads it. The sweep is drawn through the text fill.
- It holds still under `prefers-reduced-motion`, and reads as plain text in forced colours and in a
  browser without `background-clip: text`.
- The motion says "in progress" to the eye only. Pair it with words that say so, or with a status
  region, where the state matters.
